The definition below is quoted in substance from the International Neuromodulation Society page opened for this draft. Market-size figures on that page are left out.

## What the opened sources say

- The society says neuromodulation is technology that acts directly upon nerves. It is the alteration, or modulation, of nerve activity by delivering electrical or pharmaceutical agents directly to a target area.
- Examples the society names include deep brain stimulation for Parkinson's disease, sacral nerve stimulation for pelvic disorders and incontinence, spinal cord stimulation, and cochlear implants. Naming an example is not an efficacy review on this page.
- The society describes neurostimulation devices as electrodes placed at the brain, spinal cord, or peripheral nerves, connected to a pulse generator. It also describes implanted pumps that deliver a drug at the target. This page does not repeat pump-dose comparisons.

## Not stated here

Device clearances under 21 CFR Part 882, stimulation parameters, and do-it-yourself electrical devices are not specified. The tDCS, TMS, and vagus-nerve pages are stubs.

This page does not give doses, titration schedules, injection or implantation technique, preparation steps, or places to purchase a product.
