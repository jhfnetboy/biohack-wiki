This page is the wiki's editorial stance. It is not a clinical recommendation and not a ranking of products.

## What we borrow from d/acc

Vitalik Buterin's essay "My techno-optimism" (27 November 2023) names d/acc and says the "d" can stand for defense, decentralization, democracy, and differential. The 5 January 2025 follow-up, "d/acc: one year later," describes the idea as decentralized and democratic, differential defensive acceleration: build technologies that shift the offense/defense balance toward defense, without handing more power to a centralized authority.

We use that as inspiration for a health-information wiki, not as a claim that Buterin endorsed this site.

## How that shows up here

- Prefer defensive and safety-enhancing description over hype. A newer method is not automatically better evidenced.
- The wiki is open and open source. Content is [CC BY-SA 4.0](/policies/license/). Code is MIT. The [GitHub repository](https://github.com/jhfnetboy/biohack-wiki) is public, and pages link to an edit URL.
- Decision support is information for any person or organization. It is not medical advice. See the [disclaimer](/disclaimer/).
- Evidence is graded from the study design that was cited. There are no sponsored rankings.
- Informed consent and limits on neural data are part of the stance. Chile's Law 21.383, published 25 October 2021, amends the constitution so that scientific and technological development must respect physical and mental integrity and specially safeguard brain activity and information derived from it. UNESCO's Recommendation on the Ethics of Neurotechnology, adopted 11 November 2025, says collection and processing of neural data require prior, free, and informed consent except in recognized life-threatening emergencies, and it calls for safeguards against misuse.
- We do not publish dosing, injection or implantation technique, or purchasing guidance. We do not instruct coercive enhancement or covert surveillance.

## What this page does not do

It does not summarize every response to d/acc. It does not claim Chile's amendment is fully enforced, and it does not treat a UNESCO recommendation as a statute in every country.
