One hundred terms used in the twelve domains. Where a gloss points at another page, that page holds the sourced sentence.

## Active pharmaceutical ingredient

The substance intended to have a pharmacological effect in a finished drug, as distinct from excipients.

## Adverse event

An untoward medical occurrence in a person using a product. It is not, by itself, proof the product caused it.

## Assistive product

WHO uses this term for products that maintain or improve functioning. See the WHO page for the fact sheet.

## Biohacking

A loose popular label for self-directed, deliberate intervention in one’s own body and mind. This wiki does not treat the label as a method.

## Biomarker

A measured characteristic used as an indicator of a biological state. A wearable estimate is not automatically a validated biomarker.

## Brain-computer interface

A system that translates brain signals into commands for a computer or machine. Performance claims need a study.

## Breakthrough Device

A U.S. FDA device program. Eligibility and the specific device must be checked on an FDA record, which is not copied here.

## Bulk drug substance

FDA's term for an active ingredient used in compounding. Listing status has to be read from the FDA page.

## Category 2

On FDA's interim compounding lists, a category for nominated bulk substances FDA says may pose significant safety risks.

## Certificate of analysis

A manufacturer's document reporting tests on a batch. It is not an independent regulatory approval.

## CGMP

Current good manufacturing practice. The exact regulatory text depends on the product type and was not pasted here.

## ClinicalTrials.gov

The U.S. trial registry. A page on this wiki should cite an NCT record rather than summarize a trial from memory.

## Cochlear implant

An implanted device the International Neuromodulation Society names as an example of restoring a sensory function.

## Compounded drug

A drug prepared by compounding. FDA says compounded drugs are not FDA-approved.

## Continuous glucose monitor

A device that tracks glucose over time. Accuracy and whether a given product is a medical device are not stated in this glossary.

## Deep brain stimulation

Electrical stimulation through electrodes placed in deep brain targets. The neuromodulation page attributes examples to the INS.

## De Novo

A U.S. FDA pathway for novel devices. A classification decision has to be read from an FDA record.

## Do-it-yourself biology

Laboratory or community work done outside a conventional institution. This wiki does not describe how to do it.

## Electroencephalography

Recording electrical activity of the brain from electrodes. EEG is the usual abbreviation.

## Electrocorticography

Recording electrical activity from electrodes on the surface of the brain. Specifications are not given here.

## Electromyography

Recording electrical activity from muscle. Prosthetic control uses of EMG are not instructed here.

## Endotoxin

A bacterial product that can contaminate injectable drugs. Numeric limits are not stated in this glossary.

## Epigenetic clock

A model that estimates age-related state from epigenetic measurements. No clock's error is stated here.

## Evidence grade

This wiki's A–E scale, from human randomized trials down to anecdote. Unrated means the design was not cited.

## Exoskeleton

A wearable frame that supports or moves limbs. Device claims are not stated in this glossary.

## Functional near-infrared spectroscopy

An optical method for inferring brain blood-oxygen changes. fNIRS is the usual abbreviation.

## General wellness policy

An FDA policy area for low-risk products that promote a general state of health. The policy text was not pasted here.

## GLP-1

Glucagon-like peptide-1. On this wiki the drug-class notes are limited to the FDA page linked from the GLP-1 article.

## GIP

Glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide. This glossary does not assign drugs to this target.

## Glucagon

A pancreatic hormone that raises blood glucose. Receptor-agonist claims for specific drugs are not made here.

## Heart-rate variability

Variation in the time between heartbeats. A wearable's estimate needs its own validation study.

## High-performance liquid chromatography

A laboratory separation method often used in purity testing. This glossary sets no pass-fail cutoff.

## Hypoactive sexual desire disorder

The desire disorder named in the VYLEESI labeling summarized on the bremelanotide page.

## Investigational device exemption

A U.S. FDA framework for studying a significant-risk device in people. Study details are not copied here.

## Implant

A device or object placed inside the body. This wiki does not describe how to implant anything.

## Import alert

An FDA instruction to detain specified products at the border. Import Alert 66-80 has its own page.

## Informed consent

A person's agreement to an intervention or study after being told the relevant risks. Forms are not supplied here.

## Intracortical array

Electrodes that penetrate brain cortex. Channel counts and brand performance are not stated here.

## Invasiveness

How far a device enters the body. This wiki uses invasive, minimally invasive, and non-invasive as filing labels only.

## Lyophilization

Freeze-drying. This wiki does not give preparation steps for a lyophilized peptide.

## Mass spectrometry

A method that measures mass-to-charge ratios, often used to check identity. No acceptance rule is stated here.

## Medical device

A regulated product intended for diagnosis or treatment. Whether a gadget is a device depends on its claims and the law.

## Melanocortin receptor

The receptor class named on the VYLEESI labeling for bremelanotide. Subtypes are not listed here.

## Motor imagery

Imagining a movement without performing it, a paradigm used in some EEG interfaces. Accuracy is not stated.

## Myoelectric control

Using muscle electrical signals to command a prosthesis. Training steps are not described.

## NCT number

The identifier ClinicalTrials.gov assigns to a study record, in the form NCT followed by digits.

## Neurofeedback

A training setup that shows a person a signal derived from their own nervous system. Benefit claims are not stated.

## Neuromodulation

The International Neuromodulation Society's definition is summarized on the neuromodulation page.

## Neuroprosthesis

A device that replaces or assists a neural function. This glossary does not rank products.

## Nootropic

A popular label for substances used in the hope of changing cognition. It is not a regulatory category on this page.

## Off-label use

Use of an approved product for a purpose or population not in its labeling. This wiki does not recommend it.

## Orthosis

An external device that supports a body part, as distinct from a prosthesis that replaces one.

## Osseointegration

Attachment of an implant to bone. Surgical technique is not described.

## P300

An event-related brain response used in some EEG spelling interfaces. Bit rates are not stated.

## Pattern recognition control

Classifying sensor signals to choose a prosthesis movement. Algorithms are not specified.

## Peptide

A short chain of amino acids. This wiki's peptide pages do not provide doses or preparation steps.

## Pharmacovigilance

The collection of safety reports after a product is in use. A report is not automatic proof of cause.

## Premarket approval

The U.S. FDA approval route for the highest-risk devices. A PMA number has to be checked on an FDA record.

## Prosthesis

A device that replaces a missing body part. WHO's fact sheet names prosthetic limbs among assistive products.

## Quantified self

The practice of recording personal data to understand one's own life. The community site was not summarized.

## Randomized controlled trial

A study that assigns participants to groups by chance. This wiki marks evidence grade A only when such a trial is the cited design.

## Research use only

A label some sellers put on products. The U.S. notes page records one FDA warning and is not a full legal opinion.

## Resting heart rate

Heart rate measured at rest. A wearable's resting-heart-rate feature needs its own validation.

## Section 503A

A U.S. drug-compounding section of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act. The statute text was not pasted here.

## Section 503B

The U.S. section covering outsourcing facilities that compound drugs. The statute text was not pasted here.

## Cellular senescence

A cell state named as one of twelve hallmarks in the 2023 abstract on the hallmarks page.

## Senolytic

A proposed approach aimed at senescent cells. No drug is recommended, and no result is stated in this glossary.

## Spinal cord stimulation

Electrical stimulation of the spinal cord, named by the International Neuromodulation Society as a neuromodulation example.

## SSVEP

Steady-state visual evoked potential, a brain response to a flickering stimulus used in some EEG interfaces.

## Stentrode

A name used by Synchron for an endovascular interface. Specifications are on the stub device page only as an outline.

## Targeted muscle reinnervation

A surgical rerouting of nerves to muscle so that EMG signals can drive a prosthesis. Technique is not described.

## Transcranial direct current stimulation

A weak electrical current passed across the scalp. Parameters and home setups are intentionally absent.

## Transcranial magnetic stimulation

Stimulation of the brain with a magnetic coil. Cleared indications are not listed in this glossary.

## Repetitive TMS

TMS delivered as a train of pulses. Protocols are not described.

## Theta-burst stimulation

A patterned form of magnetic stimulation. Parameters are not stated.

## Transcranial alternating current stimulation

An alternating current applied through scalp electrodes. Parameters are not stated.

## Transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation

Stimulation aimed at the vagus nerve through the skin of the ear. Device settings are not stated.

## Vagus nerve stimulation

Stimulation of the vagus nerve, implanted or through the skin. Indications are not listed in this glossary.

## Utah array

An intracortical electrode array associated in public materials with Blackrock Neurotech. Specs are not copied.

## VO2max

A measure of maximal oxygen uptake during exercise. A watch estimate is not the laboratory measurement.

## Wearable

A device worn on the body that records or displays signals. Validation has to be read study by study.

## Warning letter

A public FDA letter alleging violations. The letter itself is the source, not a wiki summary.

## 510(k)

A U.S. FDA premarket notification used to claim a device is substantially equivalent to a predicate. A clearance is device-specific.

## Hallmark of aging

One of the twelve processes named in the PubMed 36599349 abstract, summarized on the hallmarks page.

## Nutrient sensing

Deregulated nutrient-sensing is one of the twelve hallmarks named in that abstract.

## Proteostasis

Protein homeostasis. Loss of proteostasis is one of the twelve hallmarks named in that abstract.

## Dysbiosis

A disturbed microbiome, named as one of the twelve hallmarks in that abstract.

## Gene therapy

A product intended to act through genetic material. FDA's cellular and gene therapy center is the U.S. starting point. Methods are not described.

## Cell therapy

A product made of cells. Clinic claims and preparation methods are not stated in this glossary.

## NFC implant

A small near-field-communication tag some people place under the skin. Placement is not described.

## RFID

Radio-frequency identification. Subdermal use is not instructed.

## Primary source

The document that states a fact: a paper, label, statute, or agency page. Wiki pages are not primary sources for each other.

## Secondary source

A summary of a primary document. This wiki labels it as secondary and does not treat it as the underlying data.

## Interstitial glucose

Glucose in the fluid between cells, which many continuous monitors estimate. It is not identical to a laboratory blood glucose.

## Pulse generator

The implanted electronics that the International Neuromodulation Society says drive stimulation leads.

## Bulks list

FDA's list of bulk drug substances that may be used in compounding when statutory conditions are met. Being discussed is not the same as being listed.

## Outsourcing facility

A U.S. compounding facility registered under section 503B. Obligations are in the statute, which was not pasted here.

## MedWatch

FDA's program for reporting adverse events and product problems. The GLP-1 page points readers to it.

## Green list

On Import Alert 66-80, FDA's list of GLP-1 API manufacturers excluded from that alert's detention recommendation. Firm names are not copied here.

## Salt form

The counter-ion attached to a drug substance. FDA says some semaglutide salts are not the approved active ingredient.
